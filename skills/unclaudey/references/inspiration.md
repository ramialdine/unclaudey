# Learning from real design (without copying it)

Distinctive work comes from studying real, shipped design and extracting *principles*: a grid, a rhythm, a type pairing logic, how a photo meets a headline. It never comes from cloning a layout, brand, or asset.

## Where designers look

**Websites (shipped, real):**
- Awwwards (awwwards.com): award-winning sites; the "Sites of the Day" archive is searchable by category.
- Godly (godly.website): curated, motion-heavy web design.
- SiteInspire (siteinspire.com): browsable by style, type, and subject.
- Land-book (land-book.com) and Lapa Ninja (lapa.ninja): landing pages by industry.
- Minimal Gallery (minimal.gallery), One Page Love (onepagelove.com), Httpster (httpster.net).

**Product UI and flows:**
- Mobbin (mobbin.com): real app and web screens and flows.
- Refero (refero.design): 140k+ real product screens with structured metadata. It has an **MCP server** agents can query (paid; https://refero.design/mcp).

**Type and imagery:**
- Fonts In Use (fontsinuse.com): typefaces in real work, by industry.
- Savee, Cosmos, Are.na: moodboards (for art direction, not layouts).

**Dribbble and Behance:** useful for concept exploration, but many shots are unbuilt concepts ("Dribbble-isation"). Don't treat them as evidence of what works in production. Don't scrape them; their terms prohibit it and the API only exposes your own shots.

## How to use a reference the user gives you

When the user shares a URL or screenshot they like:
1. Look at it: open it in a browser tool, or read the screenshot.
2. Write down 4–6 **transferable principles**. For example:
   - "full-bleed documentary photo, headline set small and low-left, lots of air"
   - "one serif display at huge size, everything else a quiet grotesk at 15px"
   - "section rhythm alternates image-left and full-width text bands"
   - "accent color sampled from the photography, used only on links"
3. State what you will **not** take: their logo, copy, illustrations, photos, exact layout, and brand colors.
4. Apply the principles to *this* brief's subject, content, and photos.

When no reference is given, don't invent one. Use the subject's own world (materials, places, vernacular) and a photographic direction from photo-archetypes.md.

## Quick self-check against templated output

Before you build, ask:
- Could this layout ship for a different company by swapping the logo? If yes, what in it is specific to *this* subject?
- Is the hero image something only this client would show?
- Did the palette come from the photos or the subject, or from habit?
- Is any section a "3 cards with icons" block that could be a photo, a real number, or a sentence instead?
